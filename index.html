About Me

Dr. Sarah Mitchell, Ph.D.

For over 12 years, I've had the privilege of walking alongside individuals and couples through their most challenging moments and greatest transformations. My approach combines evidence-based therapeutic techniques with genuine compassion and understanding.

I believe that healing happens in the context of a trusting relationship. My goal is to create a space where you feel truly seen, heard, and supported—without judgment. Together, we'll explore what's holding you back and discover the strengths you already possess.

Professional Credentials

Ph.D. in Clinical Psychology, Stanford University Licensed Clinical Psychologist (CA #PSY12345) Certified in EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing) Specialized Training in Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) Member, American Psychological Association (APA)

My Therapeutic Approach

I practice from an integrative perspective, drawing from CBT, psychodynamic therapy, mindfulness-based approaches, and attachment theory. This means I tailor my approach to fit your unique needs rather than applying a one-size-fits-all method.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Your first session is all about getting to know each other. We'll discuss what brings you to therapy, your goals, and what you hope to achieve. I'll explain my approach and answer any questions you have about the therapeutic process. There's no pressure to share more than you're comfortable with—we'll move at your pace. By the end, we'll collaboratively develop a plan for our work together.

I offer both online and in-person sessions to accommodate your preferences and schedule. Online sessions are conducted via a secure, HIPAA-compliant video platform and are just as effective as in-person therapy for most concerns. Many clients appreciate the convenience and comfort of attending from home, while others prefer the traditional in-office experience. We can discuss what works best for you.

The duration of therapy varies greatly depending on your unique situation and goals. Some clients find relief in 8-12 sessions for specific concerns, while others benefit from longer-term support lasting several months or years. We'll regularly check in about your progress and adjust our plan as needed. Therapy is ultimately your journey, and you're in control of how long you participate.

I am in-network with several major insurance providers including Blue Cross Blue Shield, Aetna, and Cigna. For other insurance plans, I can provide you with a superbill that you can submit for out-of-network reimbursement. I also offer a limited number of sliding scale spots for clients facing financial hardship. During our initial consultation, we'll discuss payment options that work for your situation.

Yes, confidentiality is a cornerstone of therapy and protected by law. I cannot share anything you tell me without your written permission, with very few exceptions: if you pose a danger to yourself or others, if there is suspected abuse of a child or vulnerable adult, or if I'm legally compelled by a court order. These situations are rare, and if any concerns arise, I'll discuss them with you openly and transparently.

If you're struggling with emotional challenges, relationship difficulties, major life transitions, or simply feel stuck, therapy can help. You don't need to have a diagnosed mental health condition to benefit from therapy—many people seek therapy for personal growth, stress management, or support during difficult times. The fact that you're considering therapy suggests you're ready to invest in your wellbeing, which is a powerful first step.
